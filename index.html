Australian Farmers' Markets

Where are Australia's farmers' markets, who can reach one, and what do they do for local producers? This page draws on the national farmers' market directory, which lists 175 markets.

Section 1 Where can you find a market?

My main source is the Australian Farmers' Markets Association directory. It holds 178 listings, which become 175 markets once duplicates are removed. New South Wales and Victoria have 70% of them. Queensland, a major food-producing state, has only thirteen.

Section 2 How far to the nearest market?

Relative to population, markets are far more common outside the capital cities. Regional Victoria and regional New South Wales have the highest density in the country, while Greater Brisbane, home to 2.8 million people, has only two. Regional towns are well served for their size; the problem is that there are few of them, and they are far apart. Click any two regions or city dots on the map to compare them.

Section 3 Where is our food produced?

Australian farms produced $70.9 billion of food and fibre in 2020–21. Meat and grain make up almost two thirds of it. Fruit, nuts and vegetables, the staples of a market stall, are under a fifth. Most Australian Farmers do not sell their produce at Farmers' Markets. For products that make up most of Australian produce, meat and grain, it is far more profitable to sell directly to commercial buyers than individual customers at a Farmers' Market

Section 4 Cost and profit for the Farmers

At a farmers' market, the producer keeps the sale price minus a stall fee, which is usually under $80. There is no wholesaler, distributor or retailer taking a cut. The best evidence on stallholders is a 2014 survey of 286 of them by the Rural Industries Research and Development Corporation. The biggest competition is the big two Australian Supermarkets: Coles and Woolworths.
